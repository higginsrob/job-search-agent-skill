One of my recent projects (still in development) I’m proudest of is a local multiplayer One Night Werewolf game I built for my family—a 3D night/day scenes, PeerJS rooms—with AI players that join the day-phase and chat alongside humans.   I've open sourced the game and distribution as a static github page: https://higginsrob.github.io/one-night-warewolf/.  The game host uses local ollama or remote API model provider credentials to enable AI Players.

What I find interesting is that humans and agents share the same conversation: accusing, defending, bluffing, and forming alliances under real game rules. The agents aren’t spectators; they have personalities, track claims, follow night/day constraints, and talk to both people and other AIs. Because Werewolf is a language game about hidden roles, they don’t just “be helpful”—they have to learn when to tell the truth and when to lie if they want to win.

The best metric is sitting next to my daughter. This game was her first real experience interacting with AI, and she loves it. Watching her argue with a bot that has a role, a strategy, and a reason to deceive her made the whole experience  much more engaging.
